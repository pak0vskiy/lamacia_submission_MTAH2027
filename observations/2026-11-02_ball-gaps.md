# 2026-11-02 · Ball blackouts: what happens inside the 41 %

Author: Maxim. Method: `analysis/02_gap_edges.py` (aggregates only; per-gap table in
`artifacts/gap_edges.csv`, local). No ground truth used. Thresholds are generic guesses, not tuned.

## TL;DR for the team

1. **When the ball vanishes, the players slow down.** Team-wide 2 s mean speed: median **2.0 m/s
   with the ball visible, 1.0 m/s with it missing**. Only ~10 % of ball-visible frames are below
   1.1 m/s; half of ball-missing frames are below 1.0.
2. **~80 % of missing-ball frames sit in gaps that contain a stoppage signature** (speed < 1.0 m/s
   somewhere inside), and ~25 % of gaps start or end with the ball on/over a line.
3. **Working hypothesis: the ball blackouts are mostly dead ball, not live play we can't see.**
   The tracker plausibly drops the ball when it goes out, is picked up, or is off-camera for a
   restart. If true, the 41 % "no ball" problem is largely a **D-detection** problem, which is
   much easier than inferring H/A without a ball.
4. **The hard core is smaller: ~15 % of missing frames** are in gaps with no restart cue
   (56 gaps, mostly short, median 5–7 s). 21 of them change nearest team across the gap: those
   are the real "possession changed while we were blind" cases.
5. **Gap edges are often contested**: the nearest-team margin at the edge is < 1.2 m in a quarter
   of gaps, so "team before vs after" is itself noisy.

## Caveats

- The stoppage cue fires if *any* 2 s inside the gap is slow. A long gap can be partly live and
  partly dead, so "gap with a cue" ≠ "gap is all D". Next step: measure per frame, not per gap.
- 1.0 m/s is a guess. Slow build-up or a keeper holding the ball can also be slow. Thresholds must
  be set on **other games**, not on this one.
- "Different team" uses 1 s of nearest-player votes at each edge; with small margins it's noisy.
- The hypothesis is not verified. Valid ways to check it: (a) other games with tracking + event
  data (ball-out / restart events); (b) the public 5 min as a **sanity check only** (do missing-ball
  frames line up with D there?), never to set thresholds.

## Implications for priorities (to discuss)

- **Move D detection up.** If most blackouts are stoppages, a good D detector covers most of the
  41 %, and the D boundaries are exactly what split possessions for spellF1.
- **Ball re-detection from video** becomes less urgent for long gaps (nothing to detect if the
  ball is out of play) but still matters for the short in-play gaps.
- **Ball-free H/A inference** is needed for ~15 % of missing frames, not 41 %. Still worth it, but
  a smaller slice.
- Within restart-cue gaps the useful question becomes *who restarts* (Smart Tagging set pieces,
  which team is near the ball at re-entry), and *where exactly the D starts and ends*.
